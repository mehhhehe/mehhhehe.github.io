Attention Is All You Need

authorsVaswani, Shazeer, Parmar, Uszkoreit, Jones, Gomez, Kaiser, Polosukhin
venueNeurIPS 2017
paperarXiv:1706.03762 →

If I had to point at a single paper sitting underneath most of what I work with today, it would be this one. It is short, and its title states its claim plainly: take the scaffolding everyone assumed you needed for sequence modelling, throw it out, and keep only attention.

The thing it removed

Before this, sequence models relied on recurrence. You read a sentence one token at a time, carrying a hidden state forward, hoping the start of the sentence still registered by the end. It worked, but it was inherently serial: token t had to wait for token t-1, and long-range dependencies got faint as they travelled down the chain. Convolutions helped, but they reached far only by stacking depth.

The Transformer's move is to let every token look directly at every other token in one step. Self-attention computes, for each position, a weighted blend of all the others, where the weights say "how much should I care about you, given what I'm trying to represent?" There is no chain to walk. The path between any two tokens is length one.

Recurrence said "remember as you go." Attention said "look at everything, and learn what to look at."

Why it mattered

Two reasons stand out. First, it parallelises. Because positions are processed together rather than in sequence, the model can use modern hardware more effectively. Second, attention turned out to be useful beyond translation. The same mechanism later appeared in models for images, audio, protein structure, and code.

The positional encoding detail is easy to skim past but worth pausing on. If you process all tokens at once, you've thrown away order, so you have to put it back: the authors inject position through fixed sinusoids added to the embeddings. It's a necessary patch for a problem the architecture created, a reminder that every design choice trades something away.

What it changed for me

The main lesson for me is that the right inductive bias can scale well: let the model decide which tokens are relevant, instead of fixing that structure by hand.

What I took away

  • Self-attention makes the path between any two tokens length one: long-range dependencies stop fading.
  • Dropping recurrence unlocked parallelism, which is most of why it scaled.
  • The real surprise was generality: one primitive, almost every modality.
  • Removing structure (order) forced an explicit fix (positional encodings). Design is always a trade.
